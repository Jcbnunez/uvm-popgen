## What Is Population Genetics?

Population genetics studies how genetic variation changes through time within populations.

A central goal is to predict evolutionary change by describing how populations transition from one generation to the next:

$$
\text{Population}(t) \rightarrow \text{Population}(t+1)
$$

The key question is:

> Given the genetic composition of a population today, what will it look like in the future?

Because inheritance and reproduction contain an element of chance, population genetics relies heavily on probability theory.

---

## Probability Refresher

Probability measures the likelihood that an event occurs.

$$
0 \le P(X) \le 1
$$

where:

- $P(X)=0$: impossible event

- $P(X)=1$: certain event

Population genetic models use probability to predict how frequently alleles and genotypes occur through time.

---

## A Simple Genetic System

Consider a locus with two alleles:

$$
A\; \text{and}\  a
$$

Define:

$$
p = \text{frequency of allele } A
$$

$$
q = \text{frequency of allele } a
$$

Because only two alleles exist:

$$
p + q = 1
$$

---

## Bernoulli Sampling

Sampling a single allele is a Bernoulli process because there are only two possible outcomes.

### Success

The sampled allele is $A$:

$$
P(A) = p
$$

### Failure

The sampled allele is $a$:

$$
P(a) = q
$$

Since these are the only possibilities:

$$
p + q = 1
$$

---

## Calculating Allele Frequencies

Allele frequencies are calculated as:

$$
p = \frac{\text{Number of }A\text{ alleles}}{\text{Total number of alleles}}
$$

### Example

Suppose we sample:

- 5 copies of $A$

- 3 copies of $a$

Then:

$$
p = \frac{5}{8} = 0.625
$$

$$
q = 1-p = 0.375
$$

---

## Predicting Future Samples

If evolutionary forces are absent, the probability of sampling an allele remains equal to its current frequency.

For example:

$$
p = 0.625
$$

Therefore:

$$
P(A) = 0.625
$$

This simple idea underlies predictive population genetics.

---

## Haploid Populations

In haploid organisms, each individual carries a single copy of every gene.

Examples:

- Bacteria

- Many fungi

If there are $N$ individuals:

$$
\text{Number of gene copies} = N
$$

Allele frequencies and individual frequencies are identical:

$$
f(A) = p
$$

$$
f(a) = q
$$

---

## Diploid Populations

Most animals and plants are diploid.

Each individual carries two copies of each chromosome.

If there are $N$ individuals:

$$
\text{Number of gene copies} = 2N
$$

Allele frequencies become:

$$
p = \frac{x_A}{2N}
$$

$$
q = \frac{x_a}{2N}
$$

where:

- $x_A$ = number of copies of allele $A$

- $x_a$ = number of copies of allele $a$

Because only two alleles exist:

$$
p + q = 1
$$

---

## Diploid Genotypes

Three genotypes are possible:

| Genotype | Description |
|-----------|-----------|
| $AA$ | Homozygous |
| $Aa$ | Heterozygous |
| $aa$ | Homozygous |

To derive genotype frequencies, assume alleles are sampled independently.

---

## Homozygote Frequencies

### AA
$$
P(AA) = p \times p = p^2
$$

### aa

$$
P(aa) = q \times q = q^2
$$

---

## Heterozygote Frequency

A heterozygote can arise in two ways:

$$
A \rightarrow a = pq
$$

$$
a \rightarrow A = qp
$$

Adding both possibilities:
$$
P(Aa) = pq + qp
$$

$$
P(Aa) = 2pq
$$

---

## Hardy-Weinberg Genotype Frequencies

The expected genotype frequencies are:
$$
AA = p^2
$$

$$
Aa = 2pq
$$

$$
aa = q^2
$$

The frequencies must sum to one:

$$
p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1
$$

This is the **Hardy-Weinberg equation**.

It can also be written as:

$$
(p+q)^2 = p^2 + 2pq + q^2
$$

Since:

$$
p + q = 1
$$

then:

$$
(p+q)^2 = 1
$$

and therefore:

$$
p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1
$$

---

## Important Equations

### Allele Frequencies
$$
p = \frac{x_A}{2N}
$$

$$
q = \frac{x_a}{2N}
$$

$$
p + q = 1
$$

### Genotype Frequencies

$$
f(AA) = p^2
$$

$$
f(Aa) = 2pq
$$

$$
f(aa) = q^2
$$

### Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium
$$
p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1
$$

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Population genetics uses probability theory to predict evolutionary change.

2. Allele frequencies are represented by $p$ and $q$.

3. Haploid populations contain $N$ gene copies.

4. Diploid populations contain $2N$ gene copies.

5. Independent sampling of alleles leads to genotype frequencies:

$$
p^2\; 2pq\; q^2
$$

6. The Hardy-Weinberg equation
$$p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$$

provides the fundamental null model for population genetics.
