# Directional Selection

## Review: Interpreting Changes in Allele Frequency

Last time we explored different possible behaviors of allele frequencies over time.

- $\Delta p = 0$ (deterministic): no evolution, Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium.

- $\Delta p > 0$: allele frequency increases.

- $\Delta p < 0$: allele frequency decreases.

- $\Delta p = 0$ (stochastic): multiple forces may balance each other.

Possible explanations include:

- Migration introducing alleles.

- Migration swamping resident alleles.

- Multiple evolutionary forces canceling each other.

- Genetic drift.

- Natural selection.

In this lecture we focus on **directional selection**.

---

# Fitness

Under stressful environmental conditions, different genotypes may have different fitness.

Fitness can be expressed as:

$$
W = m \times l
$$

where:

- $m$ = fecundity (offspring produced)

- $l$ = survival

---

# Absolute Fitness

Absolute fitness is the average number of offspring produced by a genotype.

For a two allele system:

$$
W_{ii}=m_{ii}l_{ii}
$$

$$
W_{ij}=m_{ij}l_{ij}
$$

$$
W_{jj}=m_{jj}l_{jj}
$$

Example under elevated temperature:

| Genotype | Absolute Fitness |
|-----------|-----------|
| $ii$ | 1000 |
| $ij$ | 900 |
| $jj$ | 800 |

Different genotypes exhibit different fitness values under warming conditions.

---

# Relative Fitness

Evolutionary theory is primarily concerned with **relative fitness** rather than absolute fitness.

Relative fitness is calculated as:

$$
w_i = \frac{W_i}{W_{max}}
$$

where:

- $W_i$ = absolute fitness of genotype $i$

- $W_{max}$ = highest fitness observed

Using the example above:

| Genotype | W | w |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| $ii$ | 1000 | 1.0 |
| $ij$ | 900 | 0.9 |
| $jj$ | 800 | 0.8 |

---

# Selection Coefficient

The selection coefficient measures fitness reduction relative to the most fit genotype:

$$
s_i = 1 - w_i
$$

Example:

| Genotype | Relative Fitness ($w$) | Selection Coefficient ($s$) |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| $ii$ | 1.0 | 0.0 |
| $ij$ | 1.0 | 0.0 |
| $jj$ | 0.8 | 0.2 |

Interpretation:

- Allele $i$ is favored.

- Allele $j$ is selected against.

- The heterozygote has the same fitness as $ii$.

- Allele $i$ behaves as completely dominant.

We therefore expect allele $i$ to increase in frequency over time.

---

# Forecasting Adaptive Evolution

Selection acts on Hardy-Weinberg genotype frequencies.

Expected genotype frequencies before selection:

$$
x_{ii}=p^2
$$

$$
x_{ij}=2pq
$$

$$
x_{jj}=q^2
$$

After selection:

$$
x_{ii}' = \frac{p^2 w_{ii}}{\bar w}
$$

$$
x_{ij}' = \frac{2pq w_{ij}}{\bar w}
$$

$$
x_{jj}' = \frac{q^2 w_{jj}}{\bar w}
$$

where:

$$
\bar w =
p^2 w_{ii}
+
2pq w_{ij}
+
q^2 w_{jj}
$$

is the mean population fitness.

---

# Mean Population Fitness Example

Suppose:

$$
p = 0.60
$$

Then:

$$
p^2 = 0.36
$$

$$
2pq = 0.48
$$

$$
q^2 = 0.16
$$

and

$$
w_{ii}=1
$$

$$
w_{ij}=1
$$

$$
w_{jj}=0.8
$$

Mean fitness becomes:

$$
\bar w=(0.36)(1)+(0.48)(1)+(0.16)(0.8)
$$

$$
\bar w=0.36+0.48+0.128
$$

$$
\bar w = 0.968
$$

---

# Predicting Allele Frequency After Selection

Recall:

$$
p_{t+1}=x_{ii}'+\frac{1}{2}x_{ij}'
$$

Substituting the selected genotype frequencies:

$$
p_{t+1}=\frac{p^2 w_{ii}}{\bar w}+\frac{pq w_{ij}}{\bar w}
$$

This is the fundamental equation for predicting allele frequency after one generation of directional selection.

---

# Numerical Example

Given:

$$
p=0.60
$$

$$
\bar w = 0.968
$$

Then:

$$
p_{t+1}=\frac{0.36(1)}{0.968}+\frac{0.24(1)}{0.968}
$$

Result:

$$
p_{t+1}\approx0.61
$$

Since:

$$
p_t = 0.60
$$

and

$$
p_{t+1} = 0.61
$$

the allele frequency increased.

---

# Comparison With Hardy-Weinberg

Under Hardy-Weinberg:

$$
\Delta p = 0
$$

No evolutionary change occurs.

Under directional selection:

$$
\Delta p=p_{t+1}-p_t
$$

$$
\Delta p=0.61 - 0.60
$$

$$
\Delta p=0.01
$$

Therefore the beneficial allele increases in frequency.

---

# Genotype Frequency Changes Under Selection

Using:

$$

\bar w = 0.968

$$

Selected genotype frequencies become:

$$
x_{ii}'=\frac{0.36}{0.968}=0.3719
$$

$$
x_{ij}'=\frac{0.48}{0.968}=0.4958
$$

$$
x_{jj}'=\frac{0.16(0.8)}{0.968}=0.1322
$$

Compared with the Hardy-Weinberg expectation:

| Genotype | HW | Selection |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| $ii$ | 0.36 | 0.3719 |
| $ij$ | 0.48 | 0.4958 |
| $jj$ | 0.16 | 0.1322 |

Interpretation:

- $ii$ increases.

- $ij$ increases.

- $jj$ decreases.

- Allele $i$ is favored by selection.

---

# Key Takeaways

1. Fitness differences create the opportunity for natural selection.

2. Selection acts through relative fitness, not absolute fitness.

3. Relative fitness is:

$$
w=\frac{W}{W_{max}}
$$

4. Selection coefficients are:

$$
s = 1 - w
$$

5. Mean fitness is:

$$
\bar w =p^2w_{ii}+2pqw_{ij}+q^2w_{jj}
$$

6. Allele frequency after one generation of selection is:

$$
p_{t+1}=\frac{p^2w_{ii}+pqw_{ij}}{\bar w}
$$

7. If an allele has higher fitness, its frequency will increase through time!
